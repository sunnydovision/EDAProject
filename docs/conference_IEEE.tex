\begin{abstract}
Human evaluation remains the dominant way to assess automated exploratory data analysis (AutoEDA) systems, but it is expensive, subjective, and difficult to reproduce. We introduce a reproducible automatic diagnostic framework that surfaces structural and statistical failures in AutoEDA systems---failure modes often under-emphasized when reviewers prioritize fluent prose over breakdown validity and statistical form. The framework uses 9 metrics covering correctness, structural validity, statistical quality, subspace exploration, and question-insight alignment. We apply it to three representative pipelines: a QUIS-style structured question-guided system, ONLYSTATS as a statistics-based ablation, and a free-form agentic LLM pipeline. Across Adidas US Sales, IBM Employee Attrition, and Online Sales, structured intermediate representations are associated with fewer column-type errors and broader subspace coverage in our benchmark runs. The QUIS-style pipeline achieves 94.0\% average structural validity, compared with 40.0\% for the agentic LLM, and discovers more subspace insights on average (84.4\% vs. 37.4\%). It also attains the highest Subspace Score ($x$=1.067), while the agentic LLM achieves the highest Uplift Win Rate (66.7\%, subspace beats global in 2 of 3 datasets). The QUIS-style pipeline discovers significant paradoxes in 2 of 3 datasets. Overall, across our benchmarks, schema-aware question guidance tracks higher measured structural validity and subgroup coverage than free-form prompting in these runs. More broadly, the paper contributes a reusable diagnostic framework for reproducible comparison of AutoEDA systems.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Automated exploratory data analysis (AutoEDA) systems are increasingly used to surface patterns, anomalies, and candidate hypotheses from unfamiliar datasets. As these systems become more capable, evaluating them becomes harder. Many papers still rely on human ratings or user studies, but such evaluations are expensive, subjective, and difficult to reproduce.

This challenge is especially visible in LLM-based AutoEDA pipelines. These systems can generate plausible insights even when the underlying analysis is structurally flawed, for example by using an invalid breakdown column, mismatching a pattern type to the data semantics, or missing informative subspaces. Human judgments alone often gloss over such failures, which motivates automatic diagnostic metrics that assess not only fluency, but also structural and statistical quality.

We introduce a reproducible automatic diagnostic framework for evaluating AutoEDA systems. It uses 9 metrics spanning correctness, structural validity, statistical quality, subspace exploration, and question-insight alignment. We illustrate diagnostic utility by applying the framework to three representative pipelines: a QUIS-style structured question-guided system, ONLYSTATS as a statistics-based ablation, and a free-form agentic LLM pipeline. This setup illustrates how these diagnostics discriminate the pipelines along structural validity, exploration breadth, and subspace-linked scores.

We apply the framework across three datasets (Adidas US Sales, IBM Employee Attrition, and Online Sales) to answer two questions: Can automatic diagnostics support meaningful comparison of AutoEDA systems without human raters? And what do they reveal about the design principles underlying structured versus free-form approaches?

The answer is consistent across our case study. The framework surfaces large, repeatable gaps in structural validity and subspace exploration between structured and free-form designs, showing that automatic diagnostics can meaningfully separate AutoEDA architectures without human raters. We do not claim that automatic metrics replace human evaluation in all settings; instead, they provide a reproducible diagnostic layer for studying failure modes and system differences.

Our contributions are:

\begin{itemize}
\item We introduce a reproducible automatic diagnostic framework with 9 metrics covering correctness, structural validity, statistical quality, subspace exploration, and question-insight alignment, and demonstrate application through three purposefully designed systems (structured question-guided, statistics-based ablation, and free-form agentic LLM), enabling systematic measurement of structural and statistical properties that fluency-centric human ratings often omit, alongside reproducible comparison of AutoEDA architectures.
\item Through a cross-dataset case study, we show that these diagnostics track large, repeatable contrasts between architectures without human raters, surfacing a 54-point structural-validity gap and a 47-point Subspace Rate gap between structured and free-form designs (94.0\% vs 40.0\% SVR; 84.4\% vs 37.4\% Subspace Rate; mean Subspace Score $x$=1.067; Uplift Win Rate 66.7\% for agentic LLM; significant paradoxes detected in 2 of 3 datasets).
\item In our runs, diagnostics align breakdown validity with downstream paradox detection: systems with pervasive invalid breakdowns did not yield statistically significant paradoxes under our dual-significance regime.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Automated EDA and Insight Evaluation}

Early automated EDA systems depend on statistical deviation to rank interesting findings. SEEDB~\cite{seedb} suggests visualizations by comparing target and reference distributions. QuickInsights~\cite{quickinsights} scores trends, outliers, and attributions by combined impact and significance. Zenvisage~\cite{zenvisage} allows users to retrieve views matching specified visual patterns. However, these systems do not check whether insights have the correct structural form.

Visualization-focused systems evaluate quality through perceptual and organizational criteria. Voyager~\cite{voyager} ranks views using expressiveness and effectiveness rules. Voder~\cite{voder} couples natural-language descriptions of quantitative facts with interactive visuals to prioritize findings during exploratory sessions. MetaInsight~\cite{metainsight} shapes insights into organized knowledge from data patterns. None of these check whether breakdown column types match the intended pattern.

Recent work incorporates LLMs into EDA. InsightPilot~\cite{insightpilot} uses LLMs to suggest analysis actions and is evaluated through user studies. QUIS~\cite{quis} generates guiding questions before insight search, but relies on human raters for evaluation -- an approach that is costly and hard to reproduce.

\subsection{Subgroup Analysis and Pattern Quality}

Recent methods study conditional insights alongside global aggregates. Subgroup discovery~\cite{bach} formalizes finding subsets of data whose behavior deviates systematically from the rest, often scored with measures such as weighted relative accuracy. We adapt this concept through two complementary metrics: Subspace Score measures the mean strength of subspace patterns, while Score Uplift ($\Delta$) quantifies whether subspace patterns are stronger than global ones.

Simpson's Paradox~\cite{simpson} denotes cases where a global association reverses direction within subgroups. Teng and Lin~\cite{teng} propose a kernel-based tree algorithm to detect and explain these paradoxes in observational data.

\subsection{Research Gap}

To our knowledge, no AutoEDA evaluation framework checks structural validity -- whether the breakdown column type matches the intended pattern -- alongside subgroup significance testing and paradox-aware scoring in one reproducible protocol. \textbf{Our work} addresses these omissions with a reproducible diagnostic framework comprising nine automated metrics spanning correctness, structural validity, statistical quality, subspace exploration, and question quality. In our benchmarks the agentic baseline shows pervasive structural mismatch and records zero paradoxes that meet our dual-significance SPR definition, whereas structured setups achieve the strongest SVRs and the significant paradox detections under that definition.

\section{System Overview}

To illustrate diagnostic utility, we compare three representative AutoEDA approaches that differ in two design dimensions: (a) structured versus free-form reasoning, and (b) LLM-based versus statistical selection. This controlled setup clarifies which architectural contrasts remain stable across our benchmarks once passed through these metrics.

\subsection{QUIS-Style Structured Pipeline}

This pipeline uses schema-aware question generation (QUGEN) to create Insight Cards with explicit column-type constraints, then searches for matching insights (ISGEN). Each card specifies a breakdown column (B) and measure (M); each insight is represented as a 4-tuple \textbf{(B, M, S, P)} with subspace filter S and pattern type P. ISGEN performs beam search with pattern-specific scoring to find stronger subspace patterns. This search module is shared with ONLYSTATS to enable controlled comparison of question generation strategies.

\subsection{ONLYSTATS (Ablation Baseline)}

This ablation replaces QUGEN with statistical selection: computing Kruskal-Wallis scores~\cite{kruskal} for all (B,M) pairs and selecting top-20 per breakdown. Cards are generated via templates without LLM reasoning. ONLYSTATS uses the same ISGEN search, isolating whether LLM-generated questions improve exploration beyond statistical selection.

\subsection{Free-Form Agentic LLM}

This free-form pipeline uses five sequential agents (data profiling, quality analysis, statistical analysis, pattern discovery, insight extraction) without structured representations. We implemented it using \texttt{gpt-5.4}. The LLM reasons freely over pre-computed evidence. We parse outputs into (B, M, S, P) format to test whether prompt-based reasoning achieves comparable validity to programmatic constraints.

\section{Evaluation Metrics}

\subsection{Selected Metrics}

We selected 9 essential metrics from a larger list of candidates. We group them as follows.

\subsubsection{Group 1: Correctness}

\textit{Faithfulness} checks whether the numbers in an insight can be recomputed exactly from the dataset. If an insight reports a regional total sales value, we recompute the aggregation from the CSV file and compare it to the reported figure. This metric works as a sanity check: persistently low faithfulness indicates unreliable numeric reporting, rendering companion metrics harder to interpret. A high score is expected for sound systems and makes it reasonable to interpret the following metrics as reflecting non-spurious structural and statistical contrasts.

The formula is:
\begin{equation}
\text{Faithfulness} = \frac{|\{i \in I : \text{verified}(i)\}|}{|I|}
\end{equation}

where $I$ is the set of all insights, and $\text{verified}(i)$ returns true if for all reported values $v_r$ in insight $i$, the recomputed value $v_c$ satisfies $|v_r - v_c| < \epsilon$ with $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$.

\subsubsection{Group 2: Structural Quality}

\textit{Structural Validity Rate (SVR)} checks whether the breakdown column type matches the pattern type. For example, a TREND insight should use a date or time column as the breakdown. An ATTRIBUTION insight should use a categorical breakdown, not a numeric measure column.

The formula is:
\begin{equation}
\text{SVR} = \frac{|\{i \in I : \text{valid\_breakdown}(i)\}|}{|I|}
\end{equation}

where $\text{valid\_breakdown}(i)$ checks pattern-specific rules: for TREND, the breakdown $B_i$ must be temporal; for ATTRIBUTION and DISTRIBUTION\_DIFFERENCE, $B_i$ must be categorical or ID-type; for OUTSTANDING\_VALUE, any breakdown type is valid. We report SVR overall and also by pattern type. This also ensures that breakdown-measure pairs are actionable for subgroup analysis.

\textit{Pattern Coverage} counts how many out of the four patterns appear with a valid structure. A system that only produces one or two patterns is not doing broad EDA.

The formula is:
\begin{equation}
\text{Pattern\_Coverage} = \frac{|\{p \in P : \exists i \in I, \text{type}(i) = p \land \text{valid}(i)\}|}{|P|}
\end{equation}

where $P = \{\text{TR, OV, AT, DD}\}$ are the four pattern types (TREND, OUTSTANDING\_VALUE, ATTRIBUTION, DISTRIBUTION\_DIFFERENCE), and $\text{valid}(i)$ means the insight passes SVR check.

\subsubsection{Group 3: Statistical Quality}

\textit{Statistical Significance} checks the percentage of insights that pass a statistical test at $p < 0.05$. We use pattern-specific tests: Mann-Kendall for trends \cite{mann}, z-test for outstanding values, Chi-square test for attribution \cite{mchugh}, and Kolmogorov-Smirnov test for distribution differences \cite{kolmogorov}. Effect sizes are measured using Kendall's $\tau$ for trends \cite{kendall}, z/(z+1) for outstanding values, Cramér's V for attribution \cite{cramer}, and KS statistic for distribution differences.

The formula is:
\begin{equation}
\text{Statistical\_Significance} = \frac{|\{i \in I : p\text{-value}(i) < 0.05\}|}{|I|}
\end{equation}

where $p\text{-value}(i)$ is computed using the pattern-specific test for insight $i$. Only structurally valid insights (SVR = 1) are included in this calculation.

\subsubsection{Group 4: Exploration Quality}

\textit{Subspace Rate} measures how many insights contain at least one subspace filter. A high value indicates that the system not only emits global summaries but also surfaces conditional contrasts, such as ``within Region = West''.

The formula is:
\begin{equation}
\text{Subspace\_Rate} = \frac{|\{i \in I : |S_i| > 0\}|}{|I|}
\end{equation}

where $S_i$ is the set of subspace filters for insight $i$. Each filter is a (column, value) pair that restricts the data to a subset.

\textit{Subspace Score ($x$)} measures the mean pattern strength of subspace insights, where higher values indicate stronger patterns:
\begin{equation}
x = \frac{1}{|I_S|}\sum_{i \in I_S} \text{score}(i)
\end{equation}
where $I_S = \{i \in I : |S_i| > 0\}$ and $\text{score}(i)$ is the effect size (Kendall's $\tau$ for TREND, Cramér's V for ATTRIBUTION, KS statistic for DISTRIBUTION\_DIFFERENCE, z/(z+1) for OUTSTANDING\_VALUE).

\textit{Score Uplift ($\Delta$)} compares subspace versus global pattern strength, where positive values indicate subspace insights are stronger:
\begin{equation}
\Delta = \frac{1}{|I_S|}\sum_{i \in I_S} \text{score}(i) - \frac{1}{|I_G|}\sum_{i \in I_G} \text{score}(i)
\end{equation}
where $I_G = \{i \in I : |S_i| = 0\}$. We report $x$ in aggregated results and \textit{Uplift Win Rate} (percentage of datasets where $\Delta > 0$) because averaging $\Delta$ is not meaningful when baselines differ. Per-dataset $\Delta$ values are in Tables~\ref{tab:adidas_results}--\ref{tab:online_results}.

\textit{Simpson's Paradox Rate (SPR)} detects statistically significant pattern reversals between global and subspace insights \cite{simpson}. A reversal occurs when the pattern direction changes -- for example, a positive trend globally becomes negative in a subspace. We use pattern-specific tests (Mann-Kendall for TREND, Chi-square contingency table for ATTRIBUTION, KS test for DISTRIBUTION\_DIFFERENCE) and require both global and subspace patterns to be statistically significant ($p < 0.05$).

The formula is:
\begin{equation}
\text{SPR} = \frac{|\{i \in I_S : \text{is\_paradox}(i) \land \text{is\_significant}(i)\}|}{|I_S|}
\end{equation}

where $\text{is\_paradox}(i)$ detects pattern reversal and $\text{is\_significant}(i)$ requires $p < 0.05$ for both global and subspace patterns. We report raw reversal rates per-dataset but prioritize significant paradoxes in aggregated comparison. Note that SPR is computed over subspace insights only ($I_S$), so Subspace Rate should be read alongside SPR.

\subsubsection{Group 5: Alignment and Coherence}

\textit{Question-Insight Alignment} measures how close the question text is to the final insight text. We compute cosine similarity using sentence embeddings. For example, related sentence pairs on general-domain text typically score around 0.5 with MiniLM embeddings. This metric ensures that if all systems align similarly with their input questions, differences elsewhere reflect insight quality rather than bias in the search process.

The formula is:
\begin{equation}
\text{Q-I\_Alignment} = \frac{1}{|I|} \sum_{i \in I} \text{cosine\_sim}(\text{emb}(Q_i), \text{emb}(T_i))
\end{equation}

where $Q_i$ is the question text, $T_i$ is the insight text, and $\text{emb}(\cdot)$ uses Sentence-BERT embeddings \cite{sbert}.

\textit{Reason-Insight Coherence} measures how close the reason field is to the final insight. This metric is only applicable to the structured pipeline and the agentic LLM because ONLYSTATS uses template-based questions without reason fields.

The formula is:
\begin{equation}
\text{R-I\_Coherence} = \frac{1}{|I|} \sum_{i \in I} \text{cosine\_sim}(\text{emb}(R_i), \text{emb}(T_i))
\end{equation}

where $R_i$ is the reason text for insight $i$. This metric is not applicable to ONLYSTATS.

\subsection{Cross-Dataset Analysis}

Our metrics are intertwined: breakdown validity interacts with how much subspace search can run cleanly, which in turn shapes subspace-quality readouts; we use this linkage to narrate how design choices propagate through the pipeline.

\subsubsection{Faithfulness}

All three systems achieve 100\% faithfulness across all datasets, establishing a baseline for comparing other quality dimensions. This is consistent with differences in structural validity and exploration quality not stemming from numerical errors or hallucination in these runs. Schema-aware question generation, statistical selection, and free-form reasoning all maintain correctness when computing aggregations.

\subsubsection{Structural Validity Rate}

SVR shows the largest and most consistent difference across datasets. The structured pipeline achieves 94.0\% average SVR, while the agentic LLM achieves only 40.0\% (gap of 54 percentage points). ONLYSTATS achieves 91.2\% by constraining its statistics-based selection to valid column types, helping ensure breakdown columns match pattern requirements.

The agentic LLM's core problem is systematic column-type mismatch: it records 0\% ATTRIBUTION SVR on Adidas and Online Sales, and uses categorical breakdowns for TREND in 51.5\% of Adidas cases. This reveals a key limitation: prompt-based constraints do not reliably enforce structural correctness, while programmatic validation -- through either structured generation or statistical filtering -- achieves over 90\% validity across datasets.

\subsubsection{Pattern Coverage}

All systems miss TREND on Employee Attrition and Online Sales due to limited temporal columns. The agentic LLM misses ATTRIBUTION on Adidas and Online Sales because its numeric breakdowns are structurally invalid. This limitation stems from dataset characteristics: lack of temporal columns prevents TREND analysis, while the agentic LLM's structural validity problems prevent ATTRIBUTION generation where categorical breakdowns are required.

\subsubsection{Subspace Exploration}

Subspace Rate reveals where structured approaches show the most advantage. The structured pipeline achieves 84.4\% average subspace rate (range: 79.2\%--87.2\%), compared with 37.4\% for the agentic LLM (range: 33.3\%--42.7\%) and 62.6\% for ONLYSTATS (range: 60.0\%--65.8\%).

Since the structured pipeline and ONLYSTATS share the same search module, the 21.8-point average gap between them isolates the contribution of semantically grounded questions over exhaustive enumeration. LLM-generated questions appear to focus the search on interpretable subspaces, while statistical selection produces many valid pairs where global patterns are too weak for subspace search to improve. The 47.0-point gap versus the agentic LLM reflects compounding effects: pervasive invalid breakdowns leave few admissible subspace refinements under our parsers. ONLYSTATS reaches its highest subspace rate on Online Sales (65.8\%) because exhaustive enumeration produces many valid (B,\,M) pairs on this small 9-column dataset, giving the search more entry points.

Subspace Score ($x$) and Score Uplift ($\Delta$) reveal different quality dimensions. The structured pipeline achieves the highest mean subspace score (1.067 average, peak 1.574 on Employee), but the agentic LLM achieves the highest Uplift Win Rate (66.7\%: positive $\Delta$ on Employee and Online), compared with 33.3\% for both the structured pipeline and ONLYSTATS (each positive on only one dataset). This indicates that while the structured pipeline produces stronger subspace patterns in absolute terms, the agentic LLM's subspace insights more consistently outperform its global baseline. On Adidas, all systems show negative uplift, suggesting particularly strong global patterns on this temporal dataset.

SPR reveals a distinction between raw reversal rates and significant paradoxes. While ONLYSTATS achieves the highest raw SPR (34.7\% average) compared to the structured pipeline (30.1\%), the structured pipeline is the only system to discover statistically significant paradoxes (2 of 3 datasets: Employee and Online). Both ONLYSTATS and the agentic LLM find zero significant paradoxes---consistent with raw reversal counts not guaranteeing dual-significant paradoxes under our tests, and with structurally invalid breakdowns diluting actionable subspace evidence.

\subsubsection{Statistical Significance}

Statistical significance is dataset-dependent (structured: 46.4\%, agentic LLM: 57.6\%, ONLYSTATS: 58.0\% on average). The structured pipeline leads on Adidas (83.4\%) but shows lower Statistical Significance on Employee Attrition (20.0\%), where binary/categorical variables make simpler aggregations more likely to pass significance tests under our thresholds. These contrasts stem from differing dataset geometries: temporal datasets with continuous measures favor concentrated subspace search, while categorical-heavy tables benefit more from exhaustive enumeration.

\subsubsection{Control Metrics}

Q-I Alignment is similar across the structured pipeline (0.540) and agentic LLM (0.569), consistent with comparable handling by the shared search implementation; remaining gaps therefore plausibly reflect differences in upstream inputs rather than retrieval bias alone. R-I Coherence slightly favors the structured pipeline on average (0.526 vs 0.514), though the agentic LLM scores higher on Employee Attrition (0.519 vs 0.468). This reversal is consistent with the agentic LLM's higher statistical significance on that dataset (55.8\% vs 20.0\%): when agentic LLM insights happen to be statistically stronger, their text tends to be more specific and better aligned with the reason. Neither alignment metric is reported for ONLYSTATS, which relies on templated placeholders rather than comparable question/rationale spans.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Key Findings}

Framework diagnostics highlight three repeatable contrasts. First, SVR separates the programmatic safeguards from purely prompt-guided generation by about 54 percentage points (\(\approx\)91\%+ vs.\ 40\%). The comparable SVR achieved by ONLYSTATS through statistical filtering suggests that enforcing permissible column typings---rather than nuanced prompt wording alone---explains much of what the structured pipeline attains without heavy prompt tuning. Second, Subspace Rate and Subspace Score separate a 21.8-point exploration gap between the structured pipeline and ONLYSTATS, which share the same search module, implicating question generation rather than search machinery. Uplift Win Rate further shows that the agentic LLM's subspace insights outperform its global baseline on two of three datasets (66.7\%), while the structured pipeline posts higher absolute subspace scores overall. Third, SPR distinguishes raw reversal prevalence from contrasts that satisfy our joint global--subspace significance criterion; pervasive SVR deficits on the agentic path coincide here with absent significant paradox counts---consistent with semantically mismatched breakdowns obscuring reversible subspace effects.

\subsection{Limitations}

We use \texttt{gpt-5.4} for LLM-based components; a different model may produce different exact numbers. Our embedding metrics depend on \texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2}. With only three datasets, metric differences should be read as observations rather than statistical conclusions. Nevertheless, the 54-point SVR gap and 47-point Subspace Rate gap are consistent across datasets.

\section{Conclusion}

Our three-dataset case study illustrates diagnostic utility: it flags systematic structural weaknesses in the free-form pipeline, quantifies exploration breadth and subspace-score contrasts, and---in these benchmarks---shows how breakdown validity aligns with whether reversal patterns clear joint significance thresholds. While the Structured Question-Guided Pipeline performs best on key reliability and exploration dimensions in this study, this split mainly highlights whether the diagnostics discriminate architectures among our candidates---not which design will universally generalize. The main outcome is a portable evaluation layer that can be applied to additional AutoEDA systems to support transparent, reproducible benchmarking.
